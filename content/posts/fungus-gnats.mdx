Fungus gnats are proof that something barely visible can be
genuinely infuriating.

The adults are harmless. They don't bite, they don't damage
leaves, and they die within a week. The problem is what they
leave behind — eggs in the top layer of soil that hatch into
larvae, which do damage roots, and which most people never
address before declaring victory.

That's why the gnats keep coming back.

## Why They Keep Coming Back

Most people kill the adults and stop there.

The adults are the visible part of a four-stage cycle: egg,
larva, pupa, adult. The eggs are laid in the top two to three
centimeters of moist soil. They hatch in three to six days
into larvae that feed on organic matter and, when populations
are high enough, on roots. The larvae pupate in the soil and
emerge as adults after about two weeks.

A full cycle takes three to four weeks. Treating for one week
kills the adults that were already flying. The eggs laid before
treatment hatch into a fresh generation after you've stopped.
Effective treatment runs for a minimum of four weeks and hits
multiple stages of the cycle simultaneously.

## Step 1 — Let the Soil Dry

Fungus gnat eggs and larvae need moisture to survive.

The top layer of soil drying out completely between waterings
kills eggs before they hatch and makes the environment
inhospitable for new egg-laying. This single change reduces
populations significantly within two weeks without any
other intervention.

Let the top three to four centimeters dry out completely before
watering. For most houseplants this is fine. For moisture-loving
plants like peace lilies or ferns, bottom watering solves
both problems — the surface stays dry while the roots access
water from below. Place the pot in a tray of water for
thirty minutes, then remove it and let the tray dry before
the next watering.

## Step 2 — Yellow Sticky Traps

[Yellow sticky traps](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CNGBLV5N?tag=plantproble07-20) placed at soil level
catch adults before they lay new eggs.

They don't eliminate the infestation on their own but they
reduce the breeding adult population while other treatments
address the soil. Place one trap per pot directly on the soil
surface or staked just above it. Replace when covered.

Sticky traps also serve as a monitoring tool. When the trap
catches fewer and fewer adults each week, the population is
declining. When the trap stays empty for two consecutive weeks,
the cycle is broken.

## Step 3 — BTi Soil Drench

Bacillus thuringiensis israelensis, sold as Mosquito Bits or
Gnatrol, is a naturally occurring bacteria that kills fungus
gnat larvae specifically without harming plants, beneficial
insects, or humans.

Soak [Mosquito Bits](/#affiliate-link) in water for thirty
minutes, strain out the solids, and use the infused water to
water your plants normally. The BTi settles into the soil and
kills larvae as they hatch over the following weeks. Apply once
a week for four consecutive weeks to cover the full egg-to-adult
cycle.

This is the most effective larval treatment available and the
one that breaks the cycle rather than just managing it.
Neem oil works similarly but smells significantly worse and
breaks down faster in soil.

## Step 4 — Top Dress With Sand or Perlite

After treating, add a one to two centimeter layer of coarse
sand or [perlite](perlite:https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DRGMNJHD?tag=plantproble07-20) on top of the soil.

Fungus gnats lay eggs in organic, moisture-retaining material.
Sand and perlite hold no moisture and contain no organic matter.
A top layer of either makes the surface inhospitable for
egg-laying even when the soil below is moist enough for
plant roots. This is the preventative step that stops
reinfestation after treatment ends.

## What To Do Right Now

- Stop watering every plant in the affected area until the
  top three to four centimeters of soil are completely dry.
  Do this today, before any other step
- Place yellow sticky traps at soil level in every affected
  pot. One per pot minimum
- Order Mosquito Bits or Gnatrol and start the weekly drench
  on the first watering after the soil has dried
- Run the full treatment for four weeks without stopping
  even if the adults disappear after week one. The eggs
  are still there
- Add a layer of coarse sand or perlite on top of the soil
  after treatment ends to prevent the next generation
