Spider plants are forgiving to a fault.
They survive neglect, bad light, irregular watering, and drafty
windowsills without complaint. When the tips turn brown, it's
one of the few signals they actually send.

It's almost always fixable in a single afternoon.

| What you're seeing                           | Most likely reason                   |
| -------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| Tips brown and crispy, rest of leaf healthy  | Tap water fluoride or low humidity   |
| Brown tips plus pale or yellow leaves        | Too much direct sun or underwatering |
| Tips browning on babies but not mother plant | Babies drying out faster, normal     |
| Brown tips after repotting                   | Transplant stress, resolves alone    |
| Tips brown and soft rather than crispy       | Overwatering or cold damage          |
| Brown tips on all leaves at once             | Fluoride buildup, flush the soil     |

## Tap Water

This is the cause in the majority of cases and the one people
try last instead of first.

Spider plants are sensitive to fluoride and chlorine in
municipal tap water. Both accumulate in the soil over months
of regular watering. Fluoride damages the outermost leaf cells
first, which is why the tips go before anything else. The
browning is dry and papery, starts at the very tip, and moves
inward slowly.

Switch to filtered water or let tap water sit uncovered for
24 hours before using it. The 24-hour method removes chlorine
through evaporation but doesn't remove fluoride completely.
Filtered or distilled water is the cleaner fix.

Once a month, flush the soil thoroughly with distilled water
to clear the accumulated salts and fluoride. Pour enough water
through that it drains completely from the bottom several
times. New growth after switching water sources comes in
without browning within four to six weeks.

## Low Humidity

Spider plants tolerate average household humidity reasonably
well. Below 30% the tips start showing it.

Winter is when this becomes a problem. Heating systems strip
moisture from indoor air and humidity drops well below what
the plant prefers. The tips desiccate faster than the roots
can compensate, starting at the most exposed point of each leaf.

A [small humidifier](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C2C9NHZW?tag=plantproble07-20) running nearby is the
reliable fix. Misting the leaves directly is not — it
evaporates within minutes and doesn't raise the ambient
humidity that matters. If a humidifier isn't practical,
grouping the spider plant with other plants raises the
local humidity slightly through collective transpiration.

## Direct Sun

Spider plants want bright indirect light. Direct sun through
a window, especially from the south or west in summer,
scorches the tips first.

Scorch browning is slightly different from fluoride browning.
It appears faster, sometimes within days of moving the plant
to a sunnier spot, and may affect whichever side of the leaf
faces the light source more than the other. The affected
tissue is dry and bleached-looking rather than uniformly brown.

Move the plant back from the window or add a sheer curtain
between it and the glass. New growth comes in undamaged once
the light intensity is right.

## Overwatering

Brown tips that are soft rather than crispy point to a
moisture problem rather than a humidity or water quality issue.

Overwatered spider plants develop soft, dark browning at the
tips that feels different to the touch from the papery
crispness of fluoride or humidity damage. The soil stays wet
for more than a week between waterings and the lower leaves
may also look pale or limp.

Let the soil dry out to about halfway down the pot before
watering again. Spider plants are moderately drought tolerant
and recover quickly from slight underwatering. They recover
much more slowly from root damage caused by consistently
wet soil. A [moisture meter](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09PBGLM3N?tag=plantproble07-20) takes the
guesswork out of timing.

## What To Do Right Now

- Switch to filtered or distilled water before doing anything
  else. This resolves the majority of brown tip cases within
  a month without any other changes
- Flush the soil once with distilled water if you've been
  using tap water for more than three months
- Check what's within one meter of the plant. Heating vents
  and south or west-facing windows in summer cause more
  damage than most people realize
- Feel the brown tips. Crispy and dry points to water quality
  or humidity. Soft and dark points to overwatering
- Trim existing brown tips with clean scissors at a slight
  angle to match the natural leaf shape. The plant doesn't
  need it but it makes tracking new browning much easier
